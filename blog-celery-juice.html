Celery Juice Benefits: I Tried It for 2 Weeks and This Is My Honest Review

Why I Wanted To Test Celery Juice Benefits Myself

I will be honest. When celery juice started taking over my feed, I rolled my eyes so hard I probably saw a past life. People were talking about celery juice benefits like celery had personally descended from the clouds with a clipboard. I am skeptical of anything that gets too shiny online.

But I also respect real experience more than assumption, so I tried it. For two weeks, I drank fresh celery juice in the morning and paid attention. I wanted to know what it actually did, what it did not do, and whether I would keep making it once the experiment ended.

The First Few Days Tasted Very Green

Day one was a lot. Celery juice tastes like celery with a microphone. It is salty, grassy, watery, and oddly refreshing once you stop expecting it to taste like fruit. I made mine with one large bunch of celery and a squeeze of lemon, then strained it through a nut milk bag because I do not own a juicer.

By day three, I was used to the flavor. I drank it cold from a mason jar and followed it with breakfast about 20 minutes later. I did notice that my digestion felt active, which is a polite way of saying celery does not quietly enter a room.

What Changed By The End Of Week One

By the end of the first week, the biggest change was hydration. I felt less puffy in the morning, and my skin looked a little calmer. I do not think celery juice was doing magic. I think starting my day with a mineral rich, watery vegetable drink instead of scrolling with coffee helped.

I also noticed I wanted a lighter breakfast afterward. Celery juice set a clean tone, and I naturally reached for smoothies, eggs, fruit, or yogurt instead of something heavier.

What Happened In Week Two

During week two, I stopped expecting a miracle and started appreciating the ritual. The celery juice benefits I personally noticed were subtle. My digestion felt smoother, my morning thirst was lower, and I liked the clean, salty freshness before breakfast.

What did not happen was also important. I did not suddenly glow like a glazed donut. I did not lose ten pounds. I did not become immune to stress, emails, or laundry. Celery juice is food, not a personality transplant.

What The Science Actually Supports

Celery contains antioxidants and plant compounds that may support inflammation in the body. It is mostly water, so it helps hydration. It also contains minerals like potassium and small amounts of vitamin K and folate. Some people feel digestive benefits because it is light and hydrating.

The overhyped claims are where I step back. I do not believe celery juice cures everything. I do not believe you need it to detox because your liver and kidneys are already working hard for you. I see it as a supportive morning habit, not a miracle cure.

My Exact Celery Juice Method

I wash one large bunch of celery, chop it into small pieces, and blend it with one half cup of cold water. Once it is very blended, I pour it through a nut milk bag and squeeze until the pulp is dry. Then I add a squeeze of lemon and drink it immediately over ice.

If you have a juicer, beautiful. If you do not, the blender method works. It is a little messy, but not enough to scare me away.

My Honest Verdict

Celery juice benefits are real for me, but they are quiet. I kept it in my routine a few mornings a week because I like how fresh and hydrated it makes me feel. I do not drink it every day, and I do not treat it like medicine.

Would I recommend trying it? Yes, if you are curious and you like simple morning rituals. No, if you are hoping one glass will fix a life that needs sleep, food, water, and gentleness.

Made's Note

Try celery juice with curiosity, not pressure. Let it be a clean little morning drink and nothing more dramatic than that. If the celery juice benefits show up for you, wonderful. If not, you still learned something about your body.
